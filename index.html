About

I study how multimodal models use evidence to understand intent and emotion. My interests are in multimodal reasoning, intent recognition, and affective computing.

I am a final-year Electrical and Computer Engineering student at CUHK-Shenzhen and an undergraduate researcher in the NAIS Lab. My lab PI is Prof. Fangxin Wang, and my research mentor is Dr. Dayou Zhang.

MISID: Multimodal Intent Recognition

NAIS Lab, CUHK-Shenzhen · Sep 2025 – Present

A dataset and framework for studying hidden intent in long, multimodal conversations in strategic deception games.

  • Co-developed the annotation scheme for 3,962 aligned audiovisual utterance segments and independently evaluated inter-annotator agreement.
  • Designed FRACTAM's modal decoupling and text reconstruction component for annotation and inference.
  • Contributed to model evaluation, conducted ablation studies, and co-designed the LLM-as-a-Judge protocol and FRACTAM framework.

The full framework improved mean Hidden Intent Inference Score from 34.22 to 42.79 across ten language-model backbones.

Method and evaluation details

The modal decoupling component pairs audio with eGeMAPS features and sampled video frames with FACS descriptors. It generates modality-specific descriptions without additional speech transcripts to limit textual interference and support subsequent cross-modal inconsistency analysis.

Inter-annotator agreement reached Krippendorff's α of 0.80–0.94 across structured fields. I contributed to the zero-shot evaluation of 19 language and video-language models. FRACTAM combines modality decoupling, long-range retrieval, and cross-modal evidence chains.

Team lead · 5th of 14 teams

AffectiveArt: Artwork Emotion Understanding

ACM Multimedia 2026 Grand Challenge, Track 2 · May – Jun 2026

A system for recognizing emotion in artworks and generating emotion descriptions.

  • Led a three-member team and designed the overall system architecture.
  • Fine-tuned image classifiers and designed validation-weighted voting, which outperformed the best individual model.
  • Used salience and classification outputs to condition a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3.5-27B description model.
Models and results

The classifiers used Gemma, Qwen, SigLIP2, and DINOv3. The system achieved 94.0% arousal, 88.3% valence, and 79.2% emotion accuracy on the hidden test set. Generated descriptions are outputs, not inputs to the classifiers.

Course project · Project lead

Interpretable Butterfly Classification

ECE4513 Image Processing and Computer Vision · Summer 2025

Led a four-layer CNN project to classify ten visually similar butterfly species, achieving 83.23% test accuracy. Implemented Class Activation Mapping from scratch and used PCA of feature maps to examine the image regions supporting predictions.
